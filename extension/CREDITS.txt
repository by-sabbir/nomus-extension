Training data
  GTSinger: a global multi-technique singing corpus. Zhang et al., 2024.
    https://huggingface.co/datasets/GTSinger/GTSinger (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0).
    A subset of 17 singers' phrases was converted to 16-bit mono FLAC for training.
  MUSDB18: Rafii et al., 2017. https://sigsep.github.io/datasets/musdb.html (research use).
  LibriSpeech: Panayotov et al., 2015. https://www.openslr.org/12 (CC BY 4.0).
  Free Music Archive: Defferrard et al., 2017. https://github.com/mdeff/fma (per-track Creative Commons licences).
  Medley-solos-DB: Lostanlen and Cella, 2016. https://zenodo.org/records/3464194 (CC BY 4.0).
    Solo-instrument clips (clarinet, distorted guitar, flute, piano, tenor saxophone, trumpet,
    violin) used as music-only training examples; the singer class was not used.
  URMP: Li et al., 2019. https://zenodo.org/records/5034983 (CC0 1.0).
    Separately recorded solo stems of 13 orchestral instruments, used as music-only training examples.
  Room reverberation in training is synthetic (generated in code); no impulse-response dataset is used.

Code
  The nomus engine (pkg/nomus_wasm.wasm) and extension scripts are written by the
  nomus project. The RNNoise baseline is nnnoiseless, a Rust port of Xiph.Org's
  RNNoise (BSD 3-Clause licence).
